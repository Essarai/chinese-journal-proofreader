# Full-manuscript review checklist

Use this checklist for a complete Chinese journal manuscript review. Apply only the items relevant to the manuscript and target journal.

## 1. Front matter and identifiers

- Chinese and English titles: meaning, scope, terminology, singular/plural, subtitle relationship.
- Author names and order, superscripts, affiliations, city and postal code, corresponding-author data.
- ORCID and DOI syntax, URL punctuation, fund names and grant numbers.
- Submission, revision, acceptance, and online-publication dates where present.
- Chinese and English abstracts: same research object, problem, method, findings, and conclusion; no missing numbered item.
- Chinese and English keywords: concept equivalence, number, order, capitalization, singular/plural, journal style.

## 2. Chinese language and typography

- Typos, duplicated or missing characters, homophones, malformed fixed phrases.
- Broken syntax, missing subjects or prepositions, ambiguous modifiers, overlong sentences, incomplete parallel structures.
- Terminology consistency, official names, abbreviations, and first-use definitions.
- Full-width/half-width punctuation, quotation marks, book-title marks, brackets, ranges, slashes, spaces, and mixed Chinese-English text.
- Numerals, dates, percentages, units, thousands separators, and figure/table references.
- Claims whose strength exceeds the evidence, undefined universal quantifiers, unsupported superlatives, and vague sources such as “various studies”.

## 3. Structure, argument, and consistency

- Title, abstract, headings, research questions, body, and conclusion answer the same problem.
- Numbered lists contain every promised item and retain consistent order.
- Terms and categories have stable definitions and mutually intelligible levels.
- Comparisons use the correct objects and names throughout; watch for copied names from adjacent cases.
- Recommendations follow from the diagnosed causes and evidence.
- Internal cross-references point to the correct section, table, note, or bibliography entry.

## 4. Tables, figures, notes, and appendices

- Captions, numbering, headers, row/column logic, units, totals, and source notes.
- Table cells use the same terminology and punctuation as the body.
- Notes are attached to the intended passage and do not contradict the reference list.
- Maps, images, and data-source statements meet the target journal's requirements.
- A visual problem in a renderer is distinguished from an actual Word-layout problem.

## 5. Citations and references

- Every in-text citation has a reference and every listed reference is cited, unless the journal allows otherwise.
- Citation number and cited page range correspond to the correct reference.
- Verify author order, title, publication, year, volume, issue, page range, DOI/ISBN, publisher, and place when available.
- Compare the reference list with notes and repeated citations for internal contradictions.
- Use primary sources: publisher or journal pages, Crossref/DOI records, official catalogs, laws, standards, and institutional repositories.
- If verification remains partial, comment “未完全核实” and identify the unresolved field instead of guessing.
- Confirm whether the target journal requires bilingual references, transliteration, translated titles, or a specific dash/punctuation style.

## 6. English academic text

- Preserve factual content and the author's stance; do not add results or causal claims.
- Remove literal translations, stacked nominalizations, dangling modifiers, and unnatural phrases such as “function exertion”.
- Check articles, agreement, tense, countability, capitalization, hyphenation, and parallel structure.
- Prefer accepted field terminology and concise verbs.
- When a paragraph needs substantial work, provide one coherent replacement paragraph in the comment.
- Recheck the revised English against the Chinese abstract for omissions or additions.

## 7. Time-sensitive and high-stakes statements

- Browse current official sources for laws, regulations, policies, standards, institutional names, and dates.
- Distinguish adoption, promulgation, publication, effective date, repeal, and amendment.
- If a manuscript was accepted before a later policy change but will publish after it, flag the need to update the legal or policy basis.
- Avoid turning editorial verification into legal, financial, or policy advice.

## Suggested comment labels

- `【必须修改】`: confirmed error that affects correctness.
- `【文字】`: typo, grammar, punctuation, or phrasing.
- `【格式】`: journal or document formatting.
- `【引文】`: citation/reference mismatch.
- `【事实核对】`: externally checked factual conflict.
- `【论证】`: evidence or inference boundary.
- `【英文题名】`, `【英文摘要】`, `【英文关键词】`: academic-English replacement.
- `【请作者确认】`: cannot be resolved without author or editorial input.
